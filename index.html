What most people get wrong

Property crime makes up roughly 72% of reported crime in the United States — yet it rarely leads the news or drives public debate about justice. Violent crime, which accounts for about 16%, receives vastly disproportionate media attention relative to how often it actually occurs.

This gap between what the data shows and what the public believes is one of the central tensions in criminal justice. You will return to it in Week 2 when we study how crime is measured, and again when we examine how the media frames crime.

Data note: “Property crime” includes larceny/theft, burglary, motor vehicle theft, and trespassing (NCVS 2022). “Violent crime” includes assault, robbery, rape/sexual assault, and homicide. “Drug offenses” reflect arrest data (UCR 2022) and are not directly comparable to victimization data. “White-collar/fraud” figures are significant undercounts — these crimes are rarely reported or prosecuted through the standard system. “Other” covers all remaining offense categories. Because different data sources measure different slices of crime, no single percentage is definitive — this is itself a key lesson you will explore in Week 2.
